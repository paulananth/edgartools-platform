# Mastering in market EDM products

Researched 2026-11-09. Status: research for the operator's decision on a definition of mastering. Not accepted policy. Terms follow [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md): Merge Stage, Source Record Binding, Cross-reference, Identifier Contract, Mastering Policy.

## Evidence scope and what is reused

- [Ataccama and Profisee merge rules](clean-mdm-ataccama-profisee-merge-rules-2026-09-17.md) holds the Profisee login wall (technical documentation and release notes redirect to login) and the 2026R1 vector matching. It also holds Ataccama's partitions by kind, which answer our step 2.
- [Informatica merge rules](clean-mdm-informatica-merge-rules-2026-09-17.md) is not reused here.
- [Vendor merge rules](clean-mdm-vendor-merge-rules-2026-09-17.md) holds the first Rimes search, which found no implementation-level merge rules. This note confirms that again. One correction: its link to `rimes.com/rimes-a-year-of-change-and-growth/` returns 404 on 2026-11-09, so that citation no longer resolves.

How sources were obtained, plainly:

- Each vendor's detailed technical documentation is not public. S&P EDM, Profisee and Rimes all publish marketing pages, factsheets and blog posts. None publishes its match order, tie rules or identifier rules.
- The live `spglobal.com` pages returned HTTP 403 to every fetch. The S&P page text below was read from Internet Archive copies of those same vendor pages, cited as archive URLs. The S&P and Rimes PDFs on `cdn.ihsmarkit.com` loaded directly.
- Several Rimes pages (`security-master-services`, `entity-master-services`, `identity-management-services`) redirect to `info.rimes.com`, which is a lead form. Their content was not readable. Anything only they could say is "unconfirmed".
- Quotes taken from a page through a fetch tool are marked as such when the tool, not a person, extracted them. Quote text is as returned.

## EDM, now Opus EDM

1. Definition. No page defines mastering. EDM is described as a platform that "supports the creation of a true gold copy by combining data sources from different providers" ([Data Optimization factsheet, 2022](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/0420/Factsheet-EDM-Data-Optimization.pdf)). The master data page describes "a single source of the truth for key business data" and "a single system that provides a golden copy of the truth" ([EDM Master Data Management, archive copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/products/edm-master-data-management.html)). The 2026 platform page lists "matching, mastering, enrichment and governance capabilities" and "your raw and mastered data" as separate things ([EDM overview, archive copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/solutions/products/enterprise-data-management)).
2. What arrives. Data from "internal and external" systems. The platform "identifies, validates and normalizes incoming data" ([Data Acquisition and Management factsheet, 2023](https://cdn.ihs.com/www/pdf/EDM-Data-acquisition-management.pdf)). Raw data is kept: "data is stored in raw format, allowing access to full data lineage and audit records" ([Reference master factsheet, 2023](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/EDM-Reference-Master.pdf)).
3. Finding identity. Unconfirmed for the EDM engine. The product pages read here do not say how a security or issuer is found. The only identifier-first statement is for a different offering, the managed "Entity Matching and Cross-reference Data as a Service". It uses S&P's BECRS identifiers "to cross-reference data from disparate sources", a Kensho Link AI matching engine, and says EDM is "a robust quality control mechanism for acceptance of probable matches" ([factsheet, 2024](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/0424/Entity-Matching-and-Cross-Reference-Data-as-a-Service-factsheet-final.pdf)). That is a service run by S&P, not the EDM engine's rule.
4. No identity, or two. Unconfirmed. Pages say the platform "creates exceptions" and that "the creation of new records in consuming systems can be managed by workflows" ([Reference Data page, archive copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/mi/products/edm-reference-data.html); Reference master factsheet). No page says whether a missing identity is minted, held, or sent to a steward.
5. Cross-reference identifiers. Unconfirmed. No page in this session names ISIN, CUSIP, SEDOL, FIGI or LEI for EDM. No lookup-only concept appears.
6. Survivorship. The only published statement is in an energy template, so it is not evidence for a security master: "EDM matches data from multiple sources at the attribute level, with source prioritization, data quality verifications and cross-source reference normalization" ([Well Master factsheet, 2022](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/0921/EDM_WellMaster_Factsheet.pdf)). For the product and account master: data "undergoes user-defined data quality checks before being consolidated, based on configurable business rules, to create a gold copy" ([Product and Account Master factsheet](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/1219/EDM-product-and-account-master-LR-2019.pdf)). History: "complete audit trail of any dimensional changes ... along with the effective date(s)" (same factsheet).
7. Decisions. "Data lineage (including manual and automatic overrides and enrichment that have been applied) is maintained to provide auditability of the data" (Data Acquisition and Management factsheet). The lineage page reaches "from acquisition through to matching, implementation of data quality rules, distribution and usage" (Data Optimization factsheet). Exception workflows "surface data quality issues and allow the end-user to resolve the exceptions", with "four-eyes approval" (Product and Account Master factsheet).
8. Hierarchy. EDM names a security master and "Party and issuer" ("issuer, counterparty, broker and guarantor") as separate masters and says the system "creates exceptions while mapping and maintaining relationships" (Reference Data page, archive copy). No model of issuer to security to listing is published. The documented hierarchies are the product hierarchy ([Product master factsheet](https://cdn.ihsmarkit.com/www/pdf/EDM-product-master.pdf): an "N-level investment hierarchy") and corporate hierarchy relationships in the BECRS service.

Not confirmed: whether any identifier or name rule exists in EDM configuration. The vendor says only that rules are "configurable business rules" set by the client. Opus EDM's own page adds one line: "One golden record for securities, entities, products and risk across every system you run" ([Gresham](https://www.greshamtech.com/products/opus-edm), page text as returned by a fetch tool).

## Profisee

1. Definition. Profisee defines the golden record as "a single, accurate, and consistent version of essential data about an entity" and as the result of matching, merging and survivorship ([Golden record management](https://profisee.com/platform/golden-record-management/)). A blog gives: "A golden record in MDM is a complete and accurate version of a data point stored in a master data management solution" ([What is a golden record](https://profisee.com/blog/what-is-a-golden-record/)). Both are page text as returned by a fetch tool.
2. What arrives. A source record from "disparate sources". Retention of source records is not stated: the golden record blog says other variations "would be replaced with the golden record", while the matching page says a logical merge can be pushed back to "update source systems with that same logical merge" ([Matching and survivorship](https://profisee.com/solutions/initiatives/matching-and-survivorship/)). Unconfirmed whether the originals stay linked.
3. Finding identity. [Entity resolution](https://profisee.com/solutions/initiatives/entity-resolution-software/) lists four methods: exact (deterministic), fuzzy, probabilistic and machine learning. The deterministic rule "links two records only when a trusted identifier, such as a tax ID or email address, is identical in both". The page says the process "allows an entity resolution platform to match records even when there’s no shared ID". It does not state an execution order, and it does not call name matching a last resort. Unconfirmed as an order.
4. No identity, or two. The pages say thresholds "decide which records merge and which go to your data stewards for review". Creation of a new record when nothing matches is not described on the product pages. A general guide says records that "only exist in one of the systems will be added to the master list". That is guidance on merging datasets, not documented product behavior ([Data merging guide](https://profisee.com/blog/data-merging-guide-best-practices/)). Conflicting identifiers are not addressed.
5. Cross-reference identifiers. A reference data article lists "ISIN", "CUSIP" and "SEDOL" under securities master data and "Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)" under entity identifiers ([Reference data](https://profisee.com/blog/mdm-101-reference-data/)). It does not say how they are matched or stored. The word cross-reference does not appear on the golden record page. No lookup-only identifier.
6. Survivorship. "MDM survivorship is the set of rules and logic that determines which attribute value is saved to the golden record" ([MDM survivorship](https://profisee.com/blog/mdm-survivorship/)). Strategies named: source system priority, most recent update, most complete record, data quality score, conditional rules and hybrids. The blog favors attribute-level rules. Tie handling and a per-field history of losing values are not published. Rules are "based on record completeness, recency, source trust or custom logic" (Matching and survivorship).
7. Decisions. "Full audit trails are available in the Profisee Platform for data steward review." The page says Profisee "explains every match decision" (Entity resolution). Matches are "displayed side-by-side in a match group for data stewards to review". Per-decision content (the evidence) is not described.
8. Hierarchy. Generic. "Profisee supports two types of derived hierarchies: straight-line and recursive hierarchies" ([Relationship management](https://profisee.com/platform/relationship-management/)). Financial services material lists "counterparty and entity data" and "product and instrument reference data" as domains. No page defines an issuer to security to listing model or how an issuer is linked. Unconfirmed.

Not confirmed from Profisee's own documentation, because it is behind a login (see the earlier note): execution order of methods, thresholds, match score scale, unmerge, tie rules, identifier conflict handling.

## What this means for our definition of mastering

Every vendor above stays at product-feature level in public. The comparison is therefore between our written definition and what the vendors say they do. Where a vendor is silent, the row says "silent", not "different". Words used: a source record arrives with its kind, identifiers, fields, relationships and source time.

| Step in our proposal | Agrees with the vendors | Differs from or is not settled by the vendors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 A source record arrives (kind, identifiers, fields, relationships, source time); no source writes a master directly. | All three bring data from many sources into a central hub. EDM keeps the raw format and lineage to it ("stored in raw format"). Profisee puts matching before merging. | Profisee's matching page says a merge can "update source systems with that same logical merge", so a master decision flows back to a source. Our proposal never writes to a source. EDM also allows data to be "entered manually from the EDM UI", a second way in. None describes the arrival unit as a typed record with a kind and a source time. |
| 2 Mastering classifies the kind. | EDM keeps separate masters for security, party and issuer, product and account. Rimes sells Security Master and Entity Master separately. Profisee models separate entities. Kind is a separation by domain in all three. | None describes classification as a step that decides which rules apply to an arriving record. The one documented mechanism is Ataccama's partitions, in the [existing note](clean-mdm-ataccama-profisee-merge-rules-2026-09-17.md). We keep that. |
| 3 For each identifier the record states, find the identity holding it under that identifier's Identifier Contract. | Profisee: a deterministic rule links "only when a trusted identifier ... is identical in both". S&P's cross-reference service starts from its own identifier (BECRS). Both use an identifier first. | No vendor publishes a per-identifier contract (who issues it, how many identities one value may name, how many values one identity may carry, how it was measured). Profisee's "trusted identifier" is the nearest idea and is not specified. Our contract per identifier namespace is stricter than anything published. |
| 4 One identity found: join. None: the identifier's rule mints or waits. Two, or a second value: review, never silently merged. | Uncertain or ambiguous matches go to a human in Profisee (steward review, side by side) and in EDM (exception workflows with four-eyes approval). That agrees with "review". | No vendor documents "mint or wait" per identifier. No vendor says that two identities found for one record are never merged silently; Profisee's thresholds decide "which records merge" automatically. Ataccama's rule (a bridge joins the best group and proposes the others) is documented in the existing note, and it differs from our review-without-choosing. No vendor documents a second value on one identity as a review trigger. |
| 5 Name, former name and place are a last resort, used only where the record has no other identifier. | Profisee says matching can work "even when there’s no shared ID". That is the same case. | No vendor calls name matching a last resort or restricts it to records with no other identifier. Profisee lists fuzzy and probabilistic matching as ordinary methods beside deterministic. Our rule is stricter than anything published. |
| 6 Field values are chosen by survivorship; every source record's value and its disagreement are kept. | Per-attribute survivorship by source priority, trust, recency and completeness (Profisee). Source prioritization at attribute level (EDM energy template). Lineage kept including overrides (EDM). This agrees with field-level survivorship and with keeping history. | Neither vendor publishes that losing source values and the disagreement itself are kept as data. EDM keeps raw data and an audit trail; Profisee's pages do not say. Our proposal makes the disagreement a stored fact. Also unpublished: tie handling, null handling, and whether a rank order is allowed to decide sameness (it must not in our definition, since survivorship is separate from identity). |
| 7 Every decision is recorded and explained. | Audit, lineage and steward queues are in all three: EDM ("who touched it and which rules were applied"), Profisee ("explains every match decision", audit trails), Rimes ("lineage at point of entry"). | "Explained" is not shown. No vendor publishes what a decision record holds (rule, evidence, version). Our Mastering Policy version, with a digest and proof, has no published equivalent. |

Two more points the comparison shows:

- Cross-reference. None of the three has a lookup-only identifier. Profisee and EDM treat identifiers as data on a record or as match inputs. Our Cross-reference table, where a lookup-only identifier finds but never mints or merges, is ours alone. It rests on our own measured Identifier Contracts, not on vendor practice.
- Issuer, security and listing. EDM and Rimes each keep a security master and a party, issuer or entity master as separate offerings. EDM names issuer data as "issuer, counterparty, broker and guarantor". Neither publishes how a security is joined to its issuer. Our model (a Security can exist before its issuer is known) is not contradicted, and not confirmed either.

## Open points the vendors do not settle

1. Whether a missing identity is minted, held, or sent to a steward, per identifier kind. No vendor publishes it.
2. Whether two identities found for one record are ever combined without a person. Profisee's thresholds imply automatic merging above a level; the level is unpublished.
3. What a second value for one identifier on one identity triggers.
4. Whether name matching is a last resort. No vendor says so; it is our rule only.
5. A lookup-only identifier: no vendor has the idea, so it needs our own proof.
6. Order of exact, fuzzy and probabilistic methods in Profisee. Listed, not ordered.
7. Tie rules and null rules in survivorship, and whether disagreement is kept as stored data.
8. The unit that arrives, and whether source records stay linked after a merge (Profisee's pages disagree in tone).
9. How a security links to its issuer and listing in EDM, Profisee and Rimes.
10. Whether Opus EDM keeps the EDM behavior unchanged after the January 2026 sale. The release says the product range, roadmap and delivery model continue, but that came from press coverage of the deal and was not read on a vendor page. Unconfirmed.
11. Whether Matrix IDM does any mastering at all.
