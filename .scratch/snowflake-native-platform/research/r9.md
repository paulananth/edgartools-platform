# R9: Cost and latency

Question: item 9 of ticket 01. Credits for an always-on compute pool against load windows, against today's local and AWS cost, and the latency the hop removal saves.

## Answer

**Not answerable in dollars or credits yet.** The Snowflake credit rates are not on any page we could read, and the three cost folders hold no load-window hours. Every figure below is labelled.

### Snowflake billing (from the documentation)

- Compute pools bill per node, rate set by instance family: "Credits per node, based on the instance family." Charged in IDLE, ACTIVE, STOPPING, RESIZING; not in STARTING or SUSPENDED. "Setting AUTO_SUSPEND is the main way to control spend." Billing follows the instance actually provisioned, not the configured family. https://docs.snowflake.com/en/developer-guide/snowpark-container-services/accounts-orgs-usage-views ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/developer-guide/snowpark-container-services/working-with-compute-pool
- Per-family rates: the Service Consumption Table, Table 1(c) "Snowflake Credit Table for SPCS Compute". Not obtained: the PDF is binary and our fetch could not read it. https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf ; https://snowflake.com/legal/creditconsumptiontable
- Not on the SPCS pages: billing granularity, minimum billed duration, credits per node-hour.
- Warehouses (for comparison): credits billed per second with a 60-second minimum; the dollar price of a credit is on the Pricing Guide, not the page. https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-understanding-compute
- Snowflake Postgres: compute "metered per hour in credits" by COMPUTE_FAMILY; storage "flat monthly rate per terabyte (TB)" on a byte-month basis; standard data transfer applies. Not stated: stopped or idle billing, minimum, any rate values. https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-postgres/postgres-cost
- Storage: flat rate per TB by account type (Capacity or On Demand) and region; the price is in the Pricing Guide. https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-understanding-data-storage
- Egress: "a per-byte fee for data egress" out of the account to another region or cloud; "Data transfers within the same region are free." Price in the pricing guide. https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-understanding-data-transfer

### Local and AWS figures (repo files, read only; measured in those files)

- AWS steady state about $2.00 to $2.10 a day, almost all S3; the lifecycle fix is about $1.05 a day. Source: `.scratch/ecs-cost-sizing/research/aws-steady-state-cost-and-silver-size-2026-08-14.md`.
- Per-execution cost: `load_history` $9.46 per 1,000 records; `bronze_seed_silver_gold` $0.787 per 1,000; `gold_refresh` about $0.005 per invocation. Source: `.scratch/ecs-cost-sizing/map.md`.
- Load CPU: `mdm-medium` about 17%; warehouse `medium` 100%. Source: the same map, Container Insights 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-09.
- The silver spec cites about 67 credits a week from the manifest-task incident, quoted from CLAUDE.md. It gives no credit price of its own. Source: `.scratch/silver-snowflake-compute-cost-estimate/spec.md`.

### Always-on pool against load windows

- Cost = nodes x hours x credits per node-hour. The rate is not obtained, so no dollar figure follows.
- Always-on: 730 hours a month (inference: 24 x 365 / 12).
- Load windows: their hours are not in the three folders, so they cannot be priced (not measured).
- Inference: whatever the rate, the compute line for an always-on pool equals 730 node-hours per node, and for a suspending pool equals the window hours plus the auto-suspend tail. The rate cancels in the ratio. The silver spec's 67-credits-a-week case is the cost of a pool that never suspends.

### Latency

- Measured: the loader uses about 1.7 of 8 CPU while loading; the load waits on database round trips. Source: the operator's brief, not found in the three folders.
- Inference, not measured: a pool in the same account and region as Postgres removes the internet hop from each round trip. Same-region transfer is free (documentation), so the pool-to-database path adds no egress fee inside one region.
- Not measured: the round-trip time before and after, and the load's seconds saved. A timed run is needed first.
